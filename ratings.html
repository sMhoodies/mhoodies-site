METHODOLOGY

PUBLISHED · UNCHANGED

Exit risk · 25%

Price move if the largest non-pool wallet sells its whole position into the pool, on a constant-product curve. 0% move scores 100, a 40% move scores 0. This measures risk, not proportion — a wallet holding most of a tiny float can be harmless.

Liquidity depth · 25%

Two halves, averaged. First, slippage on a $1,000 sell against the pool — can a normal position actually be exited. Second, liquidity against market cap — is the valuation backed by anything. Measuring only the second flatters tokens nobody trades; only the first flatters size.

Distribution · 25%

Holder count on a log scale to 5,000 (70%), corrected by the top 10's share of held supply (30%). Pools and burn addresses are excluded — a pool is not a community member.

Trading activity · 25%

24h volume against liquidity. Penalises both a dead book and implausible churn: below 0.5x scores down for inactivity, above 5x scores down for what is usually wash trading.

The ceiling

The average of the four is then capped by holder count against the tier table. A token cannot outrank its community size — being too small to have problems is not the same as being sound. Capped rows are marked.

What it is not

Not a recommendation, not an endorsement, not a safety guarantee. Every input is mechanical and reproducible, which makes the output checkable — not correct. A token can score well here and still go to zero.

Every figure on this page is read from Robinhood Chain and DEXScreener at page load, and nothing is stored. Click any address to check it on Blockscout — if a number here disagrees with the chain, the chain is right.
